Subject: Independent public dashboard built on SARB's published statistics — quick heads-up

Hello,

My name is Preneshen Naicker. I've built an independent, non-commercial dashboard
that visualises publicly published South African Reserve Bank statistics — policy
rates, exchange rates, money and credit, government finance, capital markets, the
external sector, and the Reserve Bank's own release register and desk notices. It's
live at:

  https://za-macro.pages.dev

I'm reaching out simply as a courtesy, not to request anything, since the project
draws on SARB's own public web endpoints rather than a published data API:

- The Web Indicators service (custom.resbank.co.za/SarbWebApi), which already sends
  a permissive cross-origin header, and
- The download-facility endpoint your own "Online Statistical Query" tool uses
  (resbank.co.za/bin/sarb/custom/downloadfacility), which I use to reach a further
  ~2,300 Quarterly Bulletin series not exposed through the Web Indicators service.

No authentication is bypassed anywhere — both are public, unauthenticated endpoints.
Requests are deliberately light: small batches (well under the server's own request-
size limit), spaced out, and an incremental design that only checks for newly
published data rather than repeatedly re-fetching full histories. A scheduled process
checks in roughly every 15 minutes for small updates; there is no bulk scraping.

The site is clearly labelled throughout as an independent, unofficial project, not
affiliated with or endorsed by the SARB, with a footer crediting "Data © South
African Reserve Bank" and a note that nothing on it constitutes financial advice.

I wanted to flag this directly rather than leave it undisclosed. If this use of the
public endpoints is a problem, or if there's a preferred or official channel I should
be using instead, please let me know and I'll adjust accordingly. Otherwise, no
response is needed — I just wanted the Bank to be aware this exists.

Thank you for the work your organisation does in publishing this data — it's made
a genuinely useful independent tool possible.

Kind regards,
